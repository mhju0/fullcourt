Rest advantage

The calculation, its evidence, and what it cannot tell us.

On this page
The questionThe comparisonModel inputsLimits

The question

Among completed regular-season games, did the more-rested team win? The headline counts games where the rested team played at home and compares its win rate with the home-team baseline.

Read against home court

Home teams already win more often. A raw win rate for rested home teams includes that advantage. The displayed difference subtracts the relevant venue baseline.

A selected season uses its own home baseline. Historical results use the baseline for the historical sample.

What enters the fatigue score

Recent workload, estimated venue-to-venue travel, time-zone terms, back-to-backs, altitude, and schedule density contribute to the score. Extended rest and overtime are also included.

The score is a model, not a direct measurement of a player’s physical fatigue.

Read the full formula and coefficient history →

What this cannot establish

These comparisons do not isolate rest from team strength and other differences between the groups. A rest advantage is not a guarantee about one game.

Missing measurements remain missing. They are not displayed as zero fatigue or zero effect.

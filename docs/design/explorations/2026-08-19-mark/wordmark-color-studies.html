2 · The color — one hue, spent everywhere the brand speaks

One constraint matters more than taste: the app's accent is indigo, spent one moment per page — it's law in the token system. If the logo takes a different hue, either the brand carries two colors (the thing the grammar forbids) or the app accent migrates with it (a bigger, product-wide change). Judged with that on the table:

Indigo — the incumbentC1

Director's recommendation: W4 + C1. All-caps FULLCOURT matches every uppercase label in the chrome (the nav tabs, REST ADVANTAGE, the stat-tile eyebrows) — the lockup joins the system instead of visiting it. The COURT half takes the brand color, which is what the nav (dimmed split) and the OG card (amber split) have each been reaching for separately — one rule replaces two accidents. And C1 indigo because the strongest color argument isn't aesthetic: the brand already owns a blue-violet, ratified and CVD-validated against the data poles. Choosing C2/C3/C6 is really choosing an app-wide accent migration — a fine thing to do deliberately, a bad thing to do as a logo side effect. NBA blue is the tempting outsider, and it costs the most: authority borrowed from the league, by a product whose entire voice is independence from hype. Once you call W and C, the next round is pure craft: the polished construction — optical corner geometry, slash terminals, the border treatment, exports.

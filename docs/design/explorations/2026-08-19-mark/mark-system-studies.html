The meaning, before the drawing

The slant already means something — it has just never been said.

The mark's own docblock calls the tilted center line "the rest-advantage differential as the shape of the court." So the honest move isn't to invent numerology (a 7°-style fact would sneak a brand number back in) — it's to ratify the mechanism: the slant is the rest advantage. A level line would mean an even game; the schedule's thumb on the scale tilts it. The static brand mark keeps the shipped, ratified tilt; on live product surfaces the angle may become literal — tilted by the actual differential of the game in view. Color carries the second meaning, inherited from the chart grammar: indigo = the brand speaking (chrome, one moment), teal/rose = the data speaking (rested/fatigued poles). A quiet mark uses indigo; a mark standing next to live data may let the poles in.

The dynamic angle — flexible by state, not just by medium

OURZI's logo changes by experience stage. FullCourt's honest equivalent: the slash tilts by the differential in view. The ratified brand tilt stays fixed in static contexts; on a game card, a loading state, or the season report, the same mark may lean by the live number. A level line = an even matchup — which means the mark itself can say "no edge tonight."

Director's read. S1 is the safest and quietest — it's today's mark with the ink removed, and it pairs perfectly with the shipped icons (same bones, different dress). S2 is the strongest identity — figure/ground does the storytelling, it survives one-color reproduction, and its half-court rung is genuinely distinctive at small sizes. S3 is the most "of the product" but the gradient slash risks reading as decoration — the poles are supposed to mean data, and here they'd mean logo. My recommendation: S2 as the companion system (front door, OG, documents, the case study), with S1 as its one-color fallback, the dynamic-angle rule adopted for product surfaces only, and the shipped icons untouched (your Option A call). If S2 earns its keep over the season, promoting it to the icons becomes a considered second decision — not a side effect.

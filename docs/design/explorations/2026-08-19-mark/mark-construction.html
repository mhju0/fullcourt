The mark, constructed

Locked: S2 split-ink · W4 caps wordmark with COURT in the color · C1 indigo. This round is the craft. Every render below is built from one construction — no stroked lines pretending to be shapes.

Slash terminals die flushThe slash is a parallelogram cut by the court's own edge — its ends are horizontal cuts along the frame, not stroke caps poking past it.
Real court proportionThe rectangle is redrawn 60×32 — the NBA court's 94×50, scaled. The mark is now literally a court, not roughly one.
Concentric cornersThe tile's radius and the court's radius are never equal: outer follows the platform, inner stays tighter, so the gap reads even.
Weight ramp per sizeAt 16px the slash and radii fatten, tones deepen; at hero sizes they relax. One drawing, four tuned cuts — spec below.
Tone replaces outlineThe empty half is a translucent panel of the ground's own ink — the border that read "MS Paint" is no longer load-bearing anywhere.
Ratified angleThe slash leans ~20.6° from vertical (12 across / 32 down on the grid) — steeper than the old 10°, the tilt you approved in S2. Dynamic surfaces may still flex it by live RA. And it is now truly centered — the centroid sits on the court's center (the imbalance you caught was inherited from the old 72-unit viewBox, not an illusion).

Construction

Grid60 × 32 units, integer endpoints only — crisp at every raster size. Court proportion = the real 94 × 50 floor.
SlashParallelogram (33,0)→(39,0)→(27,32)→(21,32): horizontal width 6, lean 20.6° from vertical, centroid pinned to the court's center (30,16) — the two panels are equal-area by construction. Ends are the court's own top and bottom edges.
HalvesFilled half ends at the slash's left edge; open half starts at its right — a 0-gap three-panel construction, no strokes.
CornersCourt rx 4 (of 32 tall). In a tile: tile radius per platform, court stays rx 4 — concentric, never matched.
ColorSlash #4F46E5 on light / #818CF8 on dark. Filled half: ground's ink. Open half: ground's ink at 6–12%.
MeaningThe slant is the rest advantage — level would mean an even game. Static cuts use this ratified tilt; product surfaces may tilt by the live differential.

The chosen finish — and the three studies it came from

Keyline MaterialP-D · THE CHOSEN CUT — P-C's hairline + P-B's finish
Technique: the concentric hairline (single stroke, 30–35% of the ground's ink, 2-unit gap, radius = court rx + gap) around the material panels — top-lit filled half, the slash darkening down its own length, tonal open half. The keyline does containment, the gradients do finish, and neither shouts. Ramp rule: the keyline drops below 24px — at favicon size it would render as fog, so the smallest cut is pure material.

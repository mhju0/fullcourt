Findings requiring a correction or decision

Homecoming road load: larger full-season impact

The scorer retains the preceding away streak on 508 homecoming team records, while the public type contract and API treat the current-away streak as zero at home. 185 home games receive a positive road-streak component.

A diagnostic removing only that home-game component, while preserving all ratified coefficients and other inputs, changes 57 rested-side/neutral classifications and 66 edge-size classifications. These are counterfactual changes, not corrected forecasts or observed results. The behavior predates the current release; prior review found no explicit decision establishing whether this homecoming carryover was intended.

Largest score effect: Lakers–Clippers, February 10. The Lakers' score changes diagnostically from 6.12 to 4.25, with the rest gap moving from +1.16 to +3.03. That changes the edge size from small to medium without changing which side is more rested.

The shared helper also leaves a previous home-stand length on 507 away-game feature records. That feature is exported for analysis but is not currently a score term. Inspect both sides of the helper when resolving the rule. Correcting only the visible road-streak label would leave the scoring inconsistency intact.

Recommendation: explicitly settle whether a road-streak term should apply on the first home game, then implement the accepted contract with regression coverage and a measured, reviewed rescore. Do not change coefficient values or silently reinterpret this as an approved carryover term.

Behind the Data: what Mileage measures

The current model uses great-circle distances between scheduled game venues. It does not observe airports, flight paths, departure/arrival times, hotels, practices, intermediate home stops or transport mode. Adding rolling seven-day values across games would double-count travel; season totals instead use one inbound leg per game.

The largest assigned inbound leg is Manchester→San Antonio, approximately 4,851 miles, into the Spurs' January 20 game after Europe. The highest venue-based total is New Orleans at approximately 55,529 miles; Toronto is lowest at approximately 35,110 miles. European fixtures explain substantial additional distance for the Pelicans/Spurs. These scales are plausible under the current endpoint assumptions, rather than evidence of actual aircraft mileage.

The 30-team airport reference remains dated research, with verified events separated from unverified candidates. This audit has not established airport choices for each season leg; a candidate is not a current exclusive team preference. Replacing arena coordinates with guessed airports would not resolve home interruptions or wrong physical venues.

Proposed Behind the Data copy: “Mileage uses straight-line distances between scheduled game venues. Actual airports, routes, ground travel and intermediate stops can differ.” Retain the simple Mileage label in the product. A future sourced-exception mechanism needs its own explanation once it exists.

What the schedule alone cannot tell us

There is no established return-home probability or universally observed geography-plus-days rule in this audit. Off dates can involve practice, media work, travel, or a genuine day off. An All-Star break may split the roster across multiple destinations. A road streak counts consecutive scheduled away games, not nights spent continuously in hotels.

The current CBA linked by the NBPA, Article XX section 9, explicitly permits qualifying Days Off outside a team's home city. Thus a game-free date does not establish a home return. The agreement does not provide a team-specific itinerary predictor.

Proposed method for a follow-up implementation

  1. Keep the reproducible direct-venue itinerary as the default while actual routing is unknown. Retain once-counted inbound Mileage separately from seven-day rolling Mileage.
  2. Store confirmed exceptions with the team, from/to game IDs, actual stop, event date, source, publication date, verification date, and whether the report covers the whole traveling party. Preserve the original assumption for comparison.
  3. For unresolved pairs, calculate direct distance, via-home distance, absolute and relative detour, gap calendar days, tip-to-tip hours, local/same-metro status, and Cup/All-Star/holiday context. Use these fields to prioritize research, not automatically insert a home stop.
  4. Show a clearly named scenario comparison in research artifacts. Neither an arbitrary distance cutoff nor a newly invented probability should feed the fatigue score without an explicit owner decision and validation.
  5. Separate physical venue location from home-court designation. Austin's Spurs games illustrate why a home-designated fixture can still require travel.

Suggested Behind the Data wording: “Mileage uses straight-line distances between scheduled game venues. We assume direct travel between consecutive games unless a documented itinerary says otherwise. Eastern-team itinerary evidence: home interruptions versus staying away

Researched 2026-10-20. This deepens the airport inventory; it makes no application, database or model changes. “Home interruption” means a team returns to its home area between two away games without an intervening home game. An individual player's injury, family visit or trade itinerary is a separate observation.

Findings

There are documented whole-team home interruptions. The strongest Eastern examples found are Bulls, November 2012; Nets, March 2016; Sixers, December 2024. They support researching geography, practice and holidays together. They do not provide frequency data, a universal time threshold, or proof of the particular Knicks Boston→New York→Los Angeles hypothesis.

The opposite behavior is also documented: teams practice or stay in an away city before the next away game. A three-calendar-day game gap can accommodate either behavior. Evidence should choose an event's route when available; otherwise both routes remain estimates.

Evidence tiers

  • T: official team report with actual team logistics or firsthand travel-coordinator testimony.
  • J: named beat/agency reporter's contemporary firsthand reporting, especially observed practice/departure or a coach's account. Journalism is primary evidence for reported events, not an official permanent team policy.
  • P: a reported future plan. Keep separate from observed completion.
  • L: secondary aggregation or search-index excerpt whose original body was not fully available; follow-up lead, not a fully accepted case.
  • H: research hypothesis. No observed itinerary is claimed.

Sources were inspected on 2026-10-20 through original pages and search-index excerpts. Basketball-Reference direct opens returned HTTP403; indexed game-log content supplied calendar corroboration where stated. Dates below distinguish the event from publication.

Knicks Boston → New York → Los Angeles hypothesis

H — not established as an observed Knicks itinerary in this bounded search. Searches covered combinations of Knicks/Boston/Los Angeles/West Coast/Greenburgh with return-home, flew-home and practice wording, alongside team reports and named beat reporting. Results frequently referred to an intervening MSG game, an individual player's medical/personal travel, or the end of a trip. Those are not accepted substitutes.

The 2024 Sixers case shows that a Northeast team can return home after Boston before heading West. It raises the hypothesis's plausibility, but the Knicks may choose earlier Western arrival, acclimation or team practice elsewhere. Minimal extra map distance also does not imply a cost-free home stop: another departure, ground transfers and reduced recovery time may matter.

Best estimate for an unsourced future occurrence: keep the chosen route labeled inferred. If timing allows meaningful home recovery and the home area lies near the onward path, prioritize the home-stop scenario for manual research. Without a dated team/beat source, preserve a direct-route alternative and disclose its effect on the estimate. Do not call the inference verified or assign a numerical probability from these few examples.

Chicago → Milwaukee: proximity does not identify where the team sleeps. The 2012 case proves home practice plus a bus, while the Clippers case proves an away-party ground continuation. For Bulls, home-to-Milwaukee is also different from a visiting club's Chicago-to-Milwaukee sequence. Research home-area return, hotel stay and travel mode separately.

Exclusions that prevent false positives

  • OG Anunoby, March2024: an NBC report, 2024-03-19 describes his medical return while teammates continued West. Individual exception; exclude from team itinerary.
  • Stephon Marbury, November2007: ESPN, 2007-12-22 concerns leaving/rejoining the team. Individual disciplinary/personal itinerary; exclude.
  • “Returned home to face…” and “home between road trips” often contain a real home game. Verify full fixture sequence before accepting an A→home→B example.
  • A nominal “five-game road trip” can contain a home interruption; the label does not establish lodging continuity.
  • Stadium occupation, home practice access and players' residences are different facts. Check a separate practice facility before treating arena occupation as a return-home prohibition.

Proposed research policy for a full-season best estimate

This is a proposed audit procedure, not an implemented algorithm or fitted behavioral model.

  1. Start with authentic fixtures and physical venues. Confirm ET game dates, actual tip times, neutral/alternate venues and reschedules. Home/away designation is not a coordinate.
  2. Enumerate consecutive-away gaps. Retain all intervals; use available hours and plausible recovery/practice windows, not a universal N-day trigger. Back-to-backs, early tips and long border transfers can constrain possibilities.
  3. Compare geography explicitly. For previous venue A, home base H, next venue B, calculate direct and via-home distance/time scenarios and the extra leg. Home along an onward path, a distant detour, and two games within one metro are different research priorities.
  4. Seek a dated movement or practice observation. Search team communications and named beat reporting around the gap. Prefer actual home/away practice, overnight location, departure or a coordinator's retrospective over schedule-preview language. Keep planned and completed distinct.
  5. Add context without making it deterministic. Holiday/family opportunity, home practice availability, arena occupation, road-practice access, cross-border formalities, weather, Cup progression and timezone acclimation are candidate explanations. A coach's stated rationale outranks an inferred one.
  6. Record each decision's provenance. Team-season, surrounding fixtures, intervening-home-game check, event/publication dates, whole-team versus subset, source tier, observed/planned/inferred state, home-stop window and unresolved alternatives.
  7. Choose an estimate with its alternatives visible. Apply documented routes case by case. For unknowns, publish the selected inferred scenario and sensitivity to the alternative. Do not backfill confidence from a green calculation or conceal an uncertain leg behind an exact mile total.

Western-team itinerary evidence: returns home between away games

Researched 2026-10-20. Scope: public historical team itineraries, with no intervening home game. This is a bounded case study, not an estimate of how often NBA teams return home. No live aircraft tracking was used. No application, database, airport mapping, or fatigue coefficient was changed.

Finding

The away/home flag in a game schedule does not establish where a team spent the interval. San Antonio supplies a reported non-All-Star home stop and a separate retrospective account of a home practice stop around All-Star. Portland and the Lakers supply road-stay counterexamples despite two non-game dates. Geography is useful for prioritizing investigation, but these cases do not establish a distance or rest-day rule.

Evidence grades below describe the itinerary claim, separately from game-calendar verification:

  • A — retrospective team account: a participant explicitly describes a completed team return, reported by a named beat reporter. This does not prove every player used the same flight.
  • B — contemporary reported team itinerary: an official team report or firsthand beat report explicitly states the team routing, stay, or practice location. Future routing remains a reported plan until corroborated retrospectively.
  • C — calendar/candidate only: games or practice participation establish a possible interval, without establishing the team's intervening location. Do not count this as a return.

Implications for FullCourt

  1. Preserve a distinction between scheduled venue sequence, reported team itinerary, individual travel, and unknown location. A normal model feature derived from consecutive game venues is a venue-to-venue proxy; these reports cannot silently turn it into actual charter miles.
  2. Use geography to flag cases for evidence collection. Useful descriptors include distance from the previous venue to home, home to the next venue, direct venue-to-venue distance, and the extra distance of routing through home: d(previous, home) + d(home, next) - d(previous, next). These describe candidate routes; none proves the chosen route or assigns a probability.
  3. Review nearby-away departures, East-to-West transitions passing near home, same-metro away labels, repeated road venues, holidays, All-Star, and known home-practice reports. Apply time feasibility to candidate review, without an automatic number-of-days threshold. A plausible low-detour return can remain unknown, and a higher-detour return can be chosen for practice or family time.
  4. Keep All-Star reset, ordinary road-trip home stop, road practice, individual split, and conditional plan as distinct evidence types. The SAS 2024 quote supports the first; it does not calibrate ordinary two-day gaps.
  5. For future model work, retain a direct-route baseline and compare explicitly labeled alternative itineraries in sensitivity analysis. Do not change ratified coefficients, production history, or team defaults from this small convenience sample.

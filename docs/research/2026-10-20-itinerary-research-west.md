# Western-team itinerary evidence: returns home between away games

Researched 2026-10-20. Scope: public historical team itineraries, with no intervening home game. This is a bounded case study, not an estimate of how often NBA teams return home. No live aircraft tracking was used. No application, database, airport mapping, or fatigue coefficient was changed.

## Finding

The away/home flag in a game schedule does not establish where a team spent the interval. San Antonio supplies a reported non-All-Star home stop and a separate retrospective account of a home practice stop around All-Star. Portland and the Lakers supply road-stay counterexamples despite two non-game dates. Geography is useful for prioritizing investigation, but these cases do not establish a distance or rest-day rule.

Evidence grades below describe the itinerary claim, separately from game-calendar verification:

- **A — retrospective team account:** a participant explicitly describes a completed team return, reported by a named beat reporter. This does not prove every player used the same flight.
- **B — contemporary reported team itinerary:** an official team report or firsthand beat report explicitly states the team routing, stay, or practice location. Future routing remains a reported plan until corroborated retrospectively.
- **C — calendar/candidate only:** games or practice participation establish a possible interval, without establishing the team's intervening location. Do not count this as a return.

## Additional corroboration and exclusions

- **Dallas at Sacramento, 2024-03-26 and 2024-03-29:** [Dwain Price's team report](https://www.nba.com/mavs/longroadtripformavs), published **2024-04-03 20:16 CDT**, describes staying in Sacramento. Its opening dates conflict with the official calendar. Use [Mar 26](https://www.nba.com/game/dal-vs-sac-0022301047) and [Mar 29](https://www.nba.com/game/dal-vs-sac-0022301073), not the article's opening chronology. The first game's official recap identifies the second as next. This is corroborating road-stay evidence, not a home-return case.
- **Golden State, January 2017:** an [AP report published Jan 20](https://www.si.com/nba/2017/01/20/ap-bkn-yoga-warriors) describes one Sacramento overnight reached by bus during an otherwise home-heavy stretch. It contains intervening home games, so it is **excluded** from the away → home → away count. It does show why a nearby road game need not mean an immediate same-night return or an aircraft leg.
- **Lakers, April 2002 Portland playoff series:** [Tim Brown's Apr 23 report](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-2002-apr-23-sp-lakernote23-story.html) describes a conditional home return before a possible Game 4. A conditional next game is not an exercised itinerary; exclude it from completed-case counts.
- **Golden State, Jan 5–8, 2016:** practice participation was reported between Lakers and Portland away games, but the retrieved reports did not establish the practice location. This remains **C**, not evidence of returning to Oakland.
- **Sacramento and LA Clippers:** this bounded search did not establish a completed whole-team home stop between consecutive away games. Airport proximity, general charter capability, a player's personal “home,” and the schedule alone do not close this gap.
- **Portland actual home reset:** not established here. The documented Portland case proves a road stay. Do not mistake absence of a found reset for evidence that the team never makes one.
- The separately assigned Eastern-team research owns the **Philadelphia 2024 Christmas Boston → Philadelphia → Utah** case. It is not duplicated or used to inflate the Western-case count here.

## Implications for FullCourt

1. Preserve a distinction between **scheduled venue sequence**, **reported team itinerary**, **individual travel**, and **unknown location**. A normal model feature derived from consecutive game venues is a venue-to-venue proxy; these reports cannot silently turn it into actual charter miles.
2. Use geography to flag cases for evidence collection. Useful descriptors include distance from the previous venue to home, home to the next venue, direct venue-to-venue distance, and the extra distance of routing through home: `d(previous, home) + d(home, next) - d(previous, next)`. These describe candidate routes; none proves the chosen route or assigns a probability.
3. Review nearby-away departures, East-to-West transitions passing near home, same-metro away labels, repeated road venues, holidays, All-Star, and known home-practice reports. Apply time feasibility to candidate review, without an automatic number-of-days threshold. A plausible low-detour return can remain unknown, and a higher-detour return can be chosen for practice or family time.
4. Keep **All-Star reset**, **ordinary road-trip home stop**, **road practice**, **individual split**, and **conditional plan** as distinct evidence types. The SAS 2024 quote supports the first; it does not calibrate ordinary two-day gaps.
5. For future model work, retain a direct-route baseline and compare explicitly labeled alternative itineraries in sensitivity analysis. Do not change ratified coefficients, production history, or team defaults from this small convenience sample.

## Verification and remaining limits

Four principal cases were checked against public team/NBA game pages and contemporary or retrospective itinerary reporting; Dallas is supplemental. The table distinguishes reported future routing from completed participant testimony. No population denominator, representative sampling, route-frequency estimate, exact takeoff time, airport, or causal decision rule was established. Weather diversions, personal emergencies, and actual private aircraft movements were not investigated.

This file is research-only. Its acceptance evidence is the linked public reporting, explicit game adjacency checks, source dates, and bounded uncertainty statements; no application tests are applicable.

# Referees — every axis, measured

Generated by `ml/referee_report.py`. Pre-registrations:
[ADR 0007](../docs/adr/0007-referee-analysis-axes-are-pre-registered.md) for axes A/B/C, `ml/referee_player_preregistration.md` (and its 2026-08-21 amendment) for D, E and F.

**Corpus.** 12,813 regular-season games with three named officials, a play stream and a box score; 499,594 foul plays; 261,988 player-games. Seasons 2015-16 … 2025-26. **No playoff games** — the cache holds the regular season only.

Every test asks the same question first: does the official dimension carry more variance than a permutation null in which each official's games are a random draw from the same seasons? That global test is multiplicity-free, and under the pre-registration **no named figure may be published from an axis whose global test does not clear p < 0.01**.

Three officials work every game and the play stream never records which one blew the whistle, so every per-official figure below is roughly **a third** of the effect it names.

The lead-size gradient, which is the largest effect anywhere in this report:

| lead | fouls called on the team that is ahead |
|---|---|
| 1-5 points | 49.62% (188,131 fouls) |

**This is not evidence of a whistle.** A trailing team attacks the rim and a leading team protects its lead, so the foul follows the basketball. The gradient is reported because it is large and real, not because it is a referee effect.

## Make-up calls: a t = 27 result, killed by its own mechanism test

Consecutive fouls switch teams **52.39%** of the time against **50.46%** from a shuffle that holds each team's foul total fixed — an excess of +1.93 points at t=+27.1, rising to +2.34 points for calls under 120 seconds apart. On its own that reads as compensation.

It is not. That null destroys the possession structure, and basketball alternates possessions. The test that separates the two uses the case where they predict **opposite signs**: an offensive foul is a turnover, so the fouling team goes straight onto defense and mechanics predicts the next foul lands on *them* — below chance — while compensation still predicts above.

| first foul | next foul switches teams | null | excess | t |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| defensive (ball changes hands) | 53.00% | 50.48% | +2.52 pp | +32.8 |
| **offensive (turnover)** | 48.47% | 50.35% | -1.88 pp | -6.4 |
| offensive, within 15 seconds | 21.84% | 50.23% | -28.39 pp | -45.0 |

The sign flips exactly where possession says it should, and within fifteen seconds of an offensive foul the next whistle goes against the **same team 78% of the time**. **The alternation is the ball changing hands, not the referee evening things up.**

The excess by gap, which traces the possession clock rather than any human intention:

| gap between calls | excess over the shuffled null |
|---|---|
| 0-15s | +2.63 pp (t=+14.6) |
| 15-30s | +5.79 pp (t=+32.0) |
| 30-60s | +1.26 pp (t=+8.5) |
| 60-120s | +0.97 pp (t=+6.8) |
| 120-300s | +0.33 pp (t=+1.9) |
| 300-10000s | -0.14 pp (t=-0.2) |

## Does the home whistle need a crowd?

2020-21 is the experiment: one season, one rulebook, one set of officials, with and without an audience.

| games | n | away share of fouls | away − home |
|---|---|---|---|
| no attendance field | 560 | 0.4998 | +0.030 |
| attendance reported | 483 | 0.5086 | +0.667 |
| crowd < 3,000 | 224 | 0.5091 | +0.723 |
| crowd >= 3,000 | 259 | 0.5082 | +0.618 |
| 2019-20 restart, no crowd | 88 | 0.5035 | +0.284 |
| 2019-20 same season, crowds | 912 | 0.5043 | +0.351 |
| every game, all seasons | 12,813 | 0.5042 | +0.339 |

One comparison suggests the whistle needs an audience and two do not. The 2019-20 restart shows no gap at all, and across all 12,139 games with an attendance figure, doubling the crowd moves the away foul share by +0.249 points at t=+1.11. **Read as a null**, with the caveat that a missing attendance field is being treated as an empty building, which is an inference rather than a fact.

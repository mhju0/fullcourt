# Does a long eastward flight on short rest cost anything?

Pre-registered in `ml/timezone_preregistration.md` before any figure below was seen.
Walk-forward, 16 blind seasons, 19,118 held-out games.
Log loss, lower is better. Protocol identical to ADR 0006.

## The raw split — home win rate by what the visitor did to get there

No controls at all. Read it for the denominators, not for the effect.

| visitor's trip | games | home win % |
|---|---:|---:|
| all games in the era | 28,876 | 58.39 |
| east ≥ 3h | 1,643 | 54.41 |
| east ≥ 3h, short rest | 458 | 53.49 |
| west ≥ 3h | 1,635 | 61.96 |
| west ≥ 3h, short rest | 428 | 66.59 |
| no long shift either way | 25,598 | 58.41 |

| model | log loss | vs the layer above |
|---|---:|---:|
| strength only | 0.62381 | — |
| four-term + strength (the baseline) | 0.62124 | +0.00258 |
| + east/west × short rest | 0.62126 | -0.00003 |

The four candidate terms together are worth **-0.00003** log loss.

For scale, from ADR 0006: every fatigue factor in the model combined is worth +0.00245,
and team strength alone is worth +0.060 — about twenty-five times as much.

## Why the raw split looks like an effect

`d_strength` reads home − away, so a positive mean means the home team was the better
side in that cell. Geography decides who flies which way: a ≥3h westward trip is an
Eastern team visiting the Pacific coast, and a ≥3h eastward trip is the reverse.

| visitor's trip | games | mean strength edge to home | home win % |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| east ≥ 3h | 1,643 | -0.0281 | 54.41 |
| west ≥ 3h | 1,635 | +0.0218 | 61.96 |
| no long shift | 25,598 | -0.0021 | 58.41 |

## What the candidates overlap with

The baseline already carries visiting altitude and back-to-back. Denver and Utah are
both western and both high, so a westward term partly restates the altitude term.

| term | games | also visiting altitude | also back-to-back |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `d_east3_short` | 458 | 0.0% | 76.2% |
| `d_west3_short` | 428 | 0.0% | 86.4% |
| `d_east3` | 1,643 | 0.0% | 21.2% |
| `d_west3` | 1,635 | 0.0% | 22.6% |

## The one-line answer

A 3+ hour eastward flight arriving on short rest costs nothing measurable. The primary term is
pinned at zero in every one of the 16 held-out seasons, and the unconstrained fit wanted it to go
the other way.

The raw east/west split is a strength confound, not jet lag: geography decides who flies which
way, and the mean strength edge to the home side flips sign with the direction. The one term that
looks stable — westward on short rest — is 86.4% back-to-backs, which the model already carries.

No `fatigue.ts` constant moved. This is **also not** evidence that the shipped
`TIME_ZONE_EASTWARD_MULTIPLIER` 1.25 / `WESTWARD` 0.85 asymmetry is correct — it found no
directional effect for that asymmetry to be about.

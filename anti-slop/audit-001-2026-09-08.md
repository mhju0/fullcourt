# Behind the Data: design and writing audit

Status: recommendations for owner review. Explore option A is approved and implemented separately. The methodology pages have not been rewritten or redesigned.

## Recommendation

Make Behind the Data an index that helps readers choose a question. Use the approved Explore tiles for that choice, at a slightly more compact size. Give each article a short explanation and a visible limitation before its optional technical material. Preserve the full evidence, formulas, sources, null results, and existing deep links.

The problem is the amount readers must process at once. Shortening every paragraph by a fixed percentage would lose useful qualifications while leaving the same page structure.

## Proposed overview

1. **Behind the Data** with one sentence: “See how each finding is calculated, where the data comes from, and what it can tell you.”
2. **Methods by topic:** seven equally weighted tiles for Rest Advantage, Schedule Edge, Playoff Rest, Player Shooting, Availability Cost, Shot Value and Officiating. Titles match the product destinations. Each has one question and a direct action such as “Read shooting methods”. Keep two columns on desktop and one on mobile; do not invent an eighth tile to fill the grid.
3. **Data sources and coverage:** a compact full-width navigation box. This is a shared reference, not another model.
4. **What the tests did not establish:** retain four short evidence links covering season win totals, travel direction, referee folklore and playoff winner selection. Qualify the scope; “no effect” would be too strong.
5. **Research archive:** a compact, explicit link to the historical referee material. Time Zones remains findable through its null-result entry and article selector.

Do not display the eleven-link reference strip and a duplicate topic grid together. Do not add search for this small, fixed collection unless observed navigation problems justify it.

## Proposed article pattern

- Title and one-sentence purpose.
- A short plain-English explanation of the metric or finding. Prefer a worked comparison using existing, source-backed values when that clarifies the method; never invent representative data.
- The most consequential limitation beside the claim, not inside a closed disclosure.
- A compact “How it is calculated” section, using a formula or definition table when appropriate.
- Optional sections named for what readers will find: “Full formula and constants”, “Validation results”, “Data sources and revisions”. Open/close immediately. Preserve URL anchors; following an anchor into collapsed content must open it.
- A clear return link to the corresponding product view. Shared evidence pages without a product counterpart return to the topic index.

Use a print stylesheet that reveals technical details. Ensure browser Find can reach disclosure content in supported browsers; preserve access when JavaScript is unavailable. Details must not turn into hidden evidence. Keep established source links and official verdict quotations verbatim.

## Humanizer examples

These are proposed local rewrites, not edits to published claims. Whole-page edits must retain supporting paragraphs, quantities and links, even if relocated.

| Before | Proposed after | Treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Data sources, calculations, evaluation methods, and limits for each analysis.” | “See how each finding is calculated, where the data comes from, and what it can tell you.” | Clear reader purpose; no new factual claim. |
| “Every figure on the surface is a coefficient from a regression on final margin.” | “Each estimate comes from a model of the final score margin.” | Introduce the meaning first; retain “regression coefficient” in technical detail. |
| “THE TEST THAT CARRIES THE VERDICT” | “Comparing officials with random assignments” | Names the procedure without courtroom language. |
| “WHERE THE ACCURACY EDGE ACTUALLY LIVES” | “Accuracy by playoff round” | Removes a stronger conclusion than the caveated comparison establishes. |
| “The base rate uses always predict the home-court team, at the historical rate they win.” | “The baseline always picks the home-court team and assigns its historical win rate as the probability.” | Repairs grammar and preserves the prediction/probability distinction. |
| “This is a null result, published on purpose: the archive does not show a consistent relationship between net edge and beating the line.” | “The archive shows no consistent relationship between net schedule edge and beating the preseason win-total line.” | Removes commentary about publication; retain the adjacent limits and the sample elsewhere. |
| “Unlike every other column, this one counts each team's season opener.” | “Worth includes season openers. Rest-day comparisons exclude them because there is no previous game.” | Explain the distinction directly; verify exact column ownership before applying across the whole page. |
| “No rest against three days off, and how much of any player's split is noise.” | “Compare shooting on no rest with three or more days of rest, with uncertainty shown.” | Matches the actual bucket and avoids equating every uncertain difference with noise. |
| “This verifies acquisition of indexed reports, not independent coverage of every eligible game.” | “We check that every report on the NBA's season index was collected. We do not independently check whether that index includes every eligible game.” | Slightly longer, easier to understand, retains the material limitation. |
| “Each report retains its source hash.” | Keep under “Source records and revisions”. | Move technical provenance rather than deleting it or substituting an imprecise promise. |

### Keep, even when trimming

Sample sizes, denominators, venue baselines, uncertainty thresholds, source dates, model-training boundaries, multiple-testing caveats, and correction records are substantive. Preserve the distinction between missing and zero. Preserve correlation versus causation and selected L2M endings versus full games. Keep retained model assumptions separate from results that tested those assumptions. Do not change coefficients, generated figures, evaluation protocols or NBA quotations during a copy pass.

## Browser and accessibility findings

At 390px, the active reference topic is off-screen on eight articles: Playoff Rest, Player Shooting, Shot Value, Availability, Officiating, Referee Effect, Time Zones and Data and Limits. The strip starts at Overview on direct arrival, regardless of the current topic. This is a navigation-orientation issue under R-03, not proof of a WCAG failure. Replace the strip with a compact topic selector or ensure the current topic is exposed immediately.

Mobile reading burden is substantial: Rest Advantage is approximately 10,116px high and Playoff Rest 7,637px. On Referee Effect the first content section starts at y=818 in an 844px viewport. Shorter titles and one navigation control would recover useful space before any substantive copy is removed.

Automated axe checks of `main` at 390px found no WCAG A/AA violations on all twelve audited routes or the local Explore implementation. None had horizontal page overflow. This does not certify the whole site or replace physical-device and screen-reader testing. Long tables/formulas retain intentional internal scrolling; preserve that keyboard access when redesigning.

Explore verification: desktop, 390px and 320px rendering; six study links; visible keyboard focus; Enter navigation to Shooting and browser Back; no JavaScript page errors. Arrow blocks denote navigation and the shallow shadow defines the pressable surface (R-08/R-12 purpose checks). Uniform tiles intentionally give the six destinations equal weight (R-14 owner choice). No motion was introduced. Existing automated scale checks caught prototype spacing that needed adaptation to the production spacing scale; implementation now uses the established values.
